# Classic Pasta Recipes Guide

## Page 1: The Art of Pasta & Base Ingredients

### 1.1 The Fundamentals of Great Pasta
Cooking perfect pasta is a fundamental culinary skill that acts as the foundation for endless variations. The golden rules include:
1. Always salt your pasta water (it should taste like the sea).
2. Cook pasta to *al dente* (firm to the bite) as it will continue cooking in the sauce.
3. Always reserve a cup of pasta water before draining; the starchy water is crucial for emulsifying sauces.

### 1.2 Choosing the Right Shape
The shape of the pasta dictates the sauce.
- Long, thin ribbons (Spaghetti, Linguine): Best with oil-based or light cream sauces.
- Tubes (Penne, Rigatoni): Excellent with heavy meat sauces or baked dishes.
- Shells (Conchiglie): Great for capturing chunky vegetable sauces or cheese.

### 1.3 Essential Italian Ingredients
Authentic Italian cooking relies on extremely high-quality, simple ingredients. Let the ingredients speak for themselves: extra virgin olive oil, Parmigiano-Reggiano, Pecorino Romano, fresh garlic, and high-quality dried pasta extruded through bronze dies.

## Page 2: Classic Spaghetti alla Carbonara

### 2.1 Overview & History
Originating from Rome, Carbonara is a rich, creamy dish made *without* cream. The creaminess comes from an emulsion of eggs, cheese, and starchy pasta water.

### 2.2 Ingredients (Serves 4)
- 400g Spaghetti or Rigatoni
- 150g Guanciale (cured pork cheek), cut into strips
- 4 large egg yolks + 1 whole egg
- 100g Pecorino Romano cheese, finely grated
- Freshly ground black pepper
- Salt (for pasta water)

### 2.3 Method
1. Place a large pot of water to boil. Salt it lightly (the guanciale and pecorino are very salty).
2. Sauté the guanciale in a cold pan over medium-low heat until the fat renders and it becomes crispy. Remove from heat.
3. In a bowl, whisk the eggs, grated pecorino, and a generous amount of black pepper.
4. Cook the pasta until al dente. Reserve pasta water.
5. Add the pasta to the pan with the guanciale fat (off the heat). Wait 30 seconds to let the pan cool slightly.
6. Pour the egg and cheese mixture over the pasta, tossing vigorously. Add splashes of pasta water as needed to create a smooth, creamy sauce. Serve immediately.

## Page 3: Spaghetti Aglio e Olio

### 3.1 Overview
"Aglio e olio" translates simply to garlic and oil. It is the quintessential late-night Italian comfort food: fast, requiring minimal pantry ingredients, yet incredibly flavorful.

### 3.2 Ingredients (Serves 2)
- 200g Spaghetti
- 4-5 cloves of garlic, thinly sliced
- 1/3 cup high-quality Extra Virgin Olive Oil
- 1 teaspoon red pepper flakes (peperoncino)
- 1/4 cup fresh parsley, finely chopped
- Salt

### 3.3 Method
1. Boil salted water and cook spaghetti until very al dente.
2. In a wide skillet, add the cold olive oil and the sliced garlic. Turn the heat to medium-low. Slowly infusing the garlic prevents it from burning and turning bitter.
3. Once the garlic gently sizzles and turns slightly golden, add the red pepper flakes and remove from heat.
4. Add 1/2 cup of pasta water to the oil (it will sputter). Swirl the pan.
5. Drain the pasta and add it to the skillet. Return to medium heat.
6. Toss the pasta vigorously while adding the fresh parsley until the oil and water emulsify into a light sauce that coats the noodles.

## Page 4: Authentic Pasta al Pesto Genovese

### 4.1 Overview
Pesto comes from Genoa in the Liguria region of Italy. The word "pesto" means "to pound," traditionally referring to the use of a marble mortar and wooden pestle.

### 4.2 Ingredients (Serves 4)
- 400g Trofie, Linguine, or Fusilli
- 50g fresh basil leaves (Genovese basil preferably), washed and dried completely
- 1/2 cup Extra Virgin Olive Oil
- 2 cloves garlic
- 2 tablespoons pine nuts (pignoli)
- 60g Parmigiano-Reggiano, grated
- 20g Pecorino Romano, grated
- A pinch of coarse sea salt

### 4.3 Method
1. If using a mortar and pestle: Crush the garlic and pine nuts with the coarse salt into a paste. Add the basil leaves gradually, grinding them against the sides in a circular motion. Add the cheeses, then slowly stream in the olive oil until a creamy sauce forms.
2. If using a food processor: Pulse the garlic and pine nuts first. Add the basil and salt, pulse again. Add the cheeses, and slowly add the oil while pulsing. Do NOT over-blend, or the heat from the blades will turn the basil dark.
3. Cook the pasta in salted water.
4. Place the pesto in a large serving bowl. Mix in a tablespoon of pasta water to loosen it.
5. Drain the pasta and toss it gently in the bowl with the raw pesto. Never cook pesto over direct heat!
