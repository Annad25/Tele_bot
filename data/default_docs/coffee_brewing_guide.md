# Comprehensive Coffee Brewing Guide

## Page 1: The Fundamentals of Brewing

### 1.1 The Golden Ratio
The coffee-to-water ratio is the mathematical foundation of any brewing method. 
- For most filter methods (Pour-over, Drip), a standard ratio is 1:16 (1 gram of coffee for every 16 grams of water).
- For immersion methods (French Press), a 1:15 ratio often feels better.
- For Espresso, the ratio is typically 1:2 (18g in, 36g out).

### 1.2 Water Temperature and Quality
A cup of coffee is 98% water. Use filtered, great-tasting water.
- Ideal brewing temperature is between 195°F and 205°F (90°C - 96°C).
- Water that is too hot will extract bitter, astringent compounds.
- Water that is too cold will result in flat, sour, under-extracted coffee.

### 1.3 Grind Size Matter
Different methods require different grind sizes to control the rate of extraction:
- **Coarse:** French Press, Cold Brew
- **Medium-Coarse:** Chemex
- **Medium:** Standard Drip, Aeropress (depending on recipe)
- **Medium-Fine:** V60, Kalita Wave
- **Fine:** Espresso
- **Extra Fine:** Turkish

## Page 2: Pour-Over (V60 & Chemex) Techniques

### 2.1 The Hario V60
The V60 is known for producing a clean, bright, and highly articulated cup of coffee.
- **Dose:** 15g coffee (Medium-Fine grind).
- **Water:** 250g.
- **The Bloom:** Pour 45g of water making sure all grounds are saturated. Wait 45 seconds to let the CO2 escape.
- **The Pour:** Pour slowly in concentric circles, avoiding the paper edges. Pour up to 150g, let it drain slightly, then pour the remaining water up to 250g. Total brew time: ~2:30 to 3:00 minutes.

### 2.2 The Chemex
The Chemex uses a very thick paper filter system that removes most of the coffee oils, resulting in a tea-like body.
- **Dose:** 30g coffee (Medium-Coarse grind).
- **Water:** 500g.
- **Method:** Similar to the V60, but adjust your pouring speed so the larger volume of water drains steadily. Expected brew time: ~4:00 to 5:00 minutes.

## Page 3: Immersion Methods: French Press & Aeropress

### 3.1 The French Press
The French press is an immersion method utilizing a metal mesh filter, which allows oils to remain in the cup for a heavy, rich body.
- **Dose:** 30g coffee (Coarse grind).
- **Water:** 450g.
- **Method:** 
  1. Add coffee and pour in all the water. 
  2. Wait 4 minutes.
  3. Break the "crust" of grounds that formed at the top with a spoon. Remove the foam.
  4. Wait another 3-5 minutes for the fines to settle to the bottom.
  5. Plunge gently and pour.

### 3.2 The Aeropress
The Aeropress is highly versatile, combining immersion with pressure.
- **Dose:** 15g coffee (Medium-Fine grind).
- **Water:** 200g.
- **Method (Inverted):** Place the plunger slightly into the chamber and flip it upside down. Add coffee, then water. Stir gently. After 2 minutes, attach the filter cap, flip the whole device onto your mug, and plunge slowly for 30 seconds.

## Page 4: Espresso Basics & Milk Steaming

### 4.1 Dialing in Espresso
Espresso is brewing under 9 bars of pressure. Due to the rapid extraction (25-30 seconds), small changes in grind size drastically affect the taste.
- **Dose:** Start with an 18g double shot.
- **Yield:** Aim for 36L liquid output.
- **Time:** Should take 25 to 30 seconds.
- *If it pulls too fast (sour), grind finer. If it pulls too slow (bitter/burnt), grind coarser.*

### 4.2 Tamping
Tamping removes air pockets and creates a level bed of coffee to provide even resistance to the water. Keep the tamper perfectly level and press until you feel the coffee push back.

### 4.3 Milk Steaming Dynamics
For lattes and cappuccinos, steaming milk perfectly means introducing micro-foam.
1. **Aeration (Stretching):** Keep the steam wand tip right at the surface of the milk to introduce air. You should hear a paper-tearing sound. (Do this while the milk is cold).
2. **Texturing (Rolling):** Submerge the wand slightly to create a vortex. This breaks the bubbles down into micro-foam, making the milk glossy like wet paint. Keep rolling until the pitcher is too hot to touch (~140°F / 60°C).
